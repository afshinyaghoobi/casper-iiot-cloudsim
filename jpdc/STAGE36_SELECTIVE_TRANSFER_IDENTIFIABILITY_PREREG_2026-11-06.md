## Formal result A: deterministic no-free-lunch under unidentifiable future MISSION drift
Let H be ALL time-causally observed CPU-finish histories through dispatch (HIGH and MISSION labels, alarm context, telemetry, all available router state). Let deterministic interval issuer f(H) be one of `abstain` or any finite interval [L,U]. Consider future MISSION error q with **no constraint at all** on its conditional distribution after H. There exist two future environments P0 and P1 whose entire histories H are identical (including arbitrarily many zero residuals in HIGH and MISSION), but P0 assigns q=0 surely and P1 assigns q=U+1 surely. If f issues [L,U] containing0, P0 covers with certainty and P1 misses with certainty. If f issues some other finite [L,U], choose a future q outside it and miss with certainty. Therefore for every history with finite issuance, worst-case conditional miscoverage over ALL possible future target laws is 1. **Distribution-free universal target-class conditional coverage <1 and nonzero guaranteed finite issuance cannot both be asserted** under unrestricted drift. Abstention is the only way to avoid a false claim without further distributional/structural assumptions; however abstention is not a safe hard-deadline scheduling action. This is a basic identifiability/no-free-lunch construction, **NOT a claimed new theorem**.

This statement is for DETERMINISTIC issuers and finite intervals, not a new result about arbitrary randomized algorithms. Never infer full scheduling noninferiority from per-task interval validity, and never call a fallback-to-greedy route a latency guarantee.

## Formal result B: class-to-class transfer requires a bound on conditional law discrepancy
Fix a measurable interval I selected **before** the future target q is realized. Suppose one has an externally validated bound `P_HIGH(q notin I | H) <= r` for the donor, and a true conditional total variation bound `TV(P_MISSION(.|H),P_HIGH(.|H)) <= delta`. Then for the same miss event, `P_MISSION(q notin I | H) <= min(1, r+delta)`. This follows immediately from TV's definition (supremum over events). A bound on just median differences, 10/10 historical MISSION samples inside donor interval, or marginal distributions without temporal conditioning is **NOT** a bound on delta. Historical HIGH empirical miss rate alone is **NOT** an externally validated r. In application, neither r nor delta is known/validated: therefore the mathematical implication is conditional and **Stage34 certificates must remain false**.

The inequality can be exactly tight. Consider discrete q in {0,20} and interval I=[-1,1]. HIGH law P(0)=0.9, P(20)=0.1; MISSION law P(0)=0.7,P(20)=0.3: r=0.1, TV=0.2, target miss=0.3 =r+delta. An even more adverse shift can occur after ten perfect MISSION labels, without violating any observed history. A coding test must also reject an estimator trying to treat empirical r or fitted delta as verified bounds.

## A transparent explicit selective scheduling *research* objective, NOT an implemented scheduling policy
For a designated target class and post-alarm epoch, define issue indicator A_t in {0,1}, signed full nonnetwork prediction interval I_t, observed later q_t, and actual deadline miss indicator M_t (from physical source). Define:
- availability `Cov = sum A_t / N`
- empirical selective prediction risk `R = sum A_t 1{q_t not in I_t} / max(1,sum A_t)` and `R=undefined` when no intervals issue, NOT 0.
- unconditional harm events per workload `H = sum A_t 1{q_t not in I_t}/N`; this can shrink just by abstaining so NEVER use alone.
- abstention frequency and cost `C_abst = sum (1-A_t)c_t/N` with explicitly stated *external cost c_t*, not inferred safety. Real deadline risk must be evaluated separately on dispatch decisions, not inferred from interval inclusion.
- Candidate composite operational loss `J = C_abst + lambda sum A_t 1{q_t not in I_t}/N` for an externally fixed lambda. Optimizing J requires independent data and a real alternative decision cost; do not claim any optimality now.

These definitions expose the tradeoff: a method that never issues has no selective-risk estimate and 100% abstention. A method issuing donor intervals after sparse MISSION may improve availability but can have 100% future miscoverage under drift. Counting 43/44 or 47/47 past selected proxy successes is no proof of future target conditional guarantee.
