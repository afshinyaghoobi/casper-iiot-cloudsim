## S1. Original A1 admission-list observation (from v2.3 §5.1)


In the 5-ms original-arrival microcase, the native scheduler displayed EXEC=[] and WAIT=[1] immediately before a later task submission; after submission it displayed EXEC=[2] and WAIT=[1]. The later-arrival configuration produced the same class of direct-submit list-state transition. Other configurations did not produce that same overtaking signature. Direct method instrumentation confirmed 54 eligibility callback invocations across the six simulations, while preserving control traces. Execution-list membership must not be equated with precise physical CPU commencement.



## S2. Retrospective A2 matched-configuration metric contrast (from v2.3 §5.6)


A **posthoc** analysis of the same 24 previously audited configurations assessed whether changing the PE capacity while retaining the same arrival family, deterministic timing variant and simulator minimum-event interval leads the two discrepancy measures to move in the same direction. There are 12 such matched pairs, each comparing a one-PE and two-PE configuration with 12 fixed tasks. All values were recomputed from the already committed original case-level results; this analysis introduces neither new native Java runs nor an independently sampled workload.

Across all 12 pairs, the maximum per-case absolute native-versus-FIFO waiting-time discrepancy was lower with two PEs. Despite this, at the synthetic submit-plus-25-ms deadline, the number of **model classification disagreements** increased in eight pairs, decreased in one and was unchanged in three; at submit-plus-50-ms, the counts increased in five, decreased in four and were unchanged in three. For example, in the BURSTY_S0_1US setting the waiting discrepancy maximum fell from **157.018 to 25.005 ms**, while the number of +25-ms deadline classification disagreements rose from **one to five of 12 tasks**. In another paired exploratory comparison, increasing minimum-event interval from 1 microsecond to 5 ms raised the within-case maximum wait discrepancy in ten of 12 matched settings but lowered it in two.

These observed crossings demonstrate a **limited metric-ordering counterexample**: a smaller maximum absolute waiting discrepancy in this fixed test grid is not guaranteed to produce fewer completion-deadline disagreements at a chosen threshold. This is an exploratory sensitivity finding, not a preregistered matched-pair hypothesis, independent confirmation, causal PE effect, physical deadline result or a new universal result about CloudSimPlus. The two response variables measure different constructs: the first is a maximum over waiting gaps, whereas the second counts differences in thresholded *completion-time* estimates. The latter depends on the job-level signed completion differences and proximity to the selected threshold. The raw task-level conditions that drive each crossing have not been causally isolated here. Reproducible audit source and the exact matched comparison are provided in the [exploratory matched-case report](SIMPAT_A2_POSTHOC_MATCHED_METRIC_DISCORDANCE_2026-11-18.md).

**Original-log, per-job margin diagnosis.** A separate source-hash-verified posthoc parser reconstructed native/FIFO completion margins for every original 288-job trace, applying the same numerical `>1e-8 ms` strict-miss decision threshold. Let `M_native(j,d)=C_native(j)-[a_j+d]` and `M_FIFO(j,d)=C_FIFO(j)-[a_j+d]` for artificial offsets `d∈{25,50} ms`. Classification differs precisely when these two margins receive opposite miss labels. Across the fixed 288 tasks, 86 threshold disagreements at +25ms comprised **73** jobs whose native finish estimate exceeded the FIFO finish and **13** with the reverse direction; the corresponding +50ms counts were **102** later-native and **5** earlier-native, total **107**. These directions are model-comparison signs, not validated physical-timing errors.

In the BURSTY_S0_1US one-PE example, only job ID **1** disagreed on +25ms; numerous other jobs had both completion estimates later than the threshold, so large timing differences did not create *classification* differences. With two PEs, IDs **2, 4, 5, 6 and 7** straddled the synthetic threshold (native finish later and FIFO finish earlier) even though maximum wait disagreement was smaller. Thus the observed ranking reversal is explained **descriptively** by threshold proximity and signed completion times, not by an inferred PE-caused physical effect. The threshold-crossing condition is algebraic, and this after-the-fact source-data analysis does **not** constitute a new prediction method or general simulation theorem. The complete job-level output, archive-hash checks and reproducible script are provided in the [original A2 per-job margin report](SIMPAT_A2_NATIVE_PERJOB_THRESHOLD_MARGIN_VERIFIED_2026-11-18.md) and [verified GitHub Actions run](https://github.com/afshinyaghoobi/casper-iiot-cloudsim/actions/runs/44352984149) (the run artifact's retention may be time-limited).



## S3. Retrospective A2 51-point artificial deadline-offset sensitivity (from v2.3 §5.7)


Although the original A2 experiment preregistered the 25- and 50-ms deadline offsets, the paired-configurations comparison and the following **51-threshold sweep were designed after those original results were observed**. To examine how much the qualitative discrepancy ranking depends on the deadline choice, we independently re-parsed the hash-verified original native A2 log, reconstructed the 288 native and FIFO completion offsets from their common observed scheduler-submission clock, and evaluated the exact same binary classification rule at every 5 ms from 0 through 250 ms, inclusive. An independent mathematical interval-membership count reproduced all 51 threshold totals. No new CloudSimPlus simulation was run.

The *number of observations on which the two model classifications disagree* is non-monotone with the deadline threshold. At the predeclared 25-ms threshold, the one-PE versus two-PE counts are **27 versus 59 of 144 fixed job-condition observations per PE group**; at 50 ms they are **49 versus 58**. By contrast, at exploratory thresholds **55 ms**, **75 ms**, and **100 ms**, they are **51 versus 47**, **64 versus 22**, and **78 versus 1**, respectively. Over the complete exploratory 51-point grid, two-PE disagreements exceed one-PE disagreements at **nine** offsets, are fewer at **40**, and tie at **two**. The largest total among these sampled offsets is **119/288 at 40 ms**; it must not be construed as a continuous global optimum or a physically meaningful IoT service-level target.

This reversal is explained by the geometry of thresholding, not proof that a PE-capacity change produces physical errors. If `L_N=C_N-a` and `L_F=C_F-a` are two per-task completion offsets from the same observed submission clock, their binary classifications differ only when the synthetic deadline offset lies between the two offsets (respecting the strict-miss numerical tolerance). Hence disagreement at an arbitrary threshold is the number of prediction-offset intervals it intersects, which need not covary monotonically with a case-level maximum absolute *waiting* discrepancy. The interval identity is elementary and the observed distributions are correlated synthetic test fixtures; neither is an original causal theorem nor evidence of field IoT deadline rates.

The complete original-source verified retrospective grid is archived as a CI derived artifact and reproducible code, with source and access limitations described in the [threshold-sensitivity report](SIMPAT_A2_POSTHOC_THRESHOLD_SENSITIVITY_VERIFIED_2026-11-18.md). This investigation reinforces a bounded **measurement-reporting recommendation**: declare synthetic deadline definitions and examine sensitivity across plausible offsets rather than extrapolating from isolated threshold choices. Establishing a novel generalizable simulator-validation method or model correctness still requires independent evidence.



## S4. Passive waiting-promotion instrumentation (from v2.3 §5.8)


To address a specific observability limitation of the original six-case A1 admission study, we **prospectively specified an additional passive instrumentation method after the original six-case outcomes were already known**. We reran the *same* original six deterministic Java cases on CloudSimPlus 8.5.7, leaving input arrivals, CPU lengths and minimum-event intervals unchanged. A read-only subclass logs before and after the exact source method `moveNextCloudletsFromWaitingToExecList(currentTime)` and calls its superclass implementation exactly once. After removing only the new method-log records, the original eligibility, submission, processing-update and completion trace was **byte-identical** to a separately rerun original A1 Java control. The newly instrumented experiment also repeated byte-for-byte; all six cases completed.

Across the six cases the new hook observed **33 method invocations**, including **10 individual invocations with at least one observed WAIT-to-EXEC membership transition**. The two previously identified configurations with a younger submitted job entering the EXEC list while an older job remained in WAIT each had a later, directly observed waiting-promotion call: in `ORIGINAL_5MS`, younger job 2 entered EXEC on submission at **1013.0997406 ms**, while older job 1 was subsequently promoted by the separate method at **1037.2200236 ms**; in `LATE10_5MS`, the corresponding times were **1023.0997406 ms** and **1058.099623 ms**. Intermediate promotion-method calls left job 1 waiting while job 2 was listed as executing. Those state transitions and clock values were obtained from the native Java execution, not deduced from the final completion order or static source text.

This method-level evidence demonstrates that direct admission and deferred waiting promotion are **separate executed runtime paths under the fixed observed conditions**, improving the interpretation of the A1 anomaly. It does **not** establish which event callback originally caused a particular capacity/eligibility decision, prove an actual hardware CPU start, assert a scheduler contract violation, or causally attribute the 24-case A2 native-versus-FIFO residuals to one source method. The additional method observation is an explicitly **post-outcome-motivated six-case diagnostic**, not an independent prospective holdout or real IoT experiment. [Original native source, archival trace and control-equivalence audit](SIMPAT_NATIVE_A1_WAIT_PROMOTION_HOOK_VERIFIED_2026-11-18.md); [successful native CI run](https://github.com/afshinyaghoobi/casper-iiot-cloudsim/actions/runs/44359114031).



## S5. Posthoc source-event reconstruction (from v2.3 §5.9)


A further **posthoc reconstruction** combined the previously archived original six-case Java trace records from the now-instrumented `updateProcessing`, `moveNextCloudletsFromWaitingToExecList`, and `canExecuteCloudletInternal` methods. The reanalysis was executed independently from the original promotion-method result parser, after the six-case outcomes were known, and required byte-identical equivalence of the original passive control trace after removing only the additional method-log lines. The specific event-order sequence described below was present in **two of six** original deterministic conditions (`ORIGINAL_5MS` and `LATE10_5MS`) and absent as a complete sequence in the other four. These are original reused test conditions, not six new independent samples.

In both positive conditions, the scheduler's processing-update method began with job 0 recorded in `EXEC` and older job 1 in `WAIT`. The **actual waiting-promotion method executed** at **1013.099623 ms** and the recorded eligibility check for job 1 returned **false** while job 0 still occupied the execution list. The waiting-promotion call returned without transferring job 1. At the end of that processing update the execution list was empty, yet job 1 remained waiting. In `ORIGINAL_5MS`, job 2 was submitted only **0.0001176 ms** later (at 1013.0997406 ms); its eligibility returned **true** and the direct submit method inserted job 2 into `EXEC` while job 1 remained in `WAIT`. In `LATE10_5MS`, the corresponding submit occurred **10.0001176 ms** after the update (at 1023.0997406 ms), with the same contrasting observed eligibility states. Later separate waiting-promotion calls moved job 1 into `EXEC` at 1037.2200236 and 1058.099623 ms, respectively.

This source-executed sequence supplies a **specific event-phase observability condition** that is relevant when simplifying native SpaceShared behavior into an analytical FIFO queue: a deferred waiting-job eligibility check may occur before the end of a processing update that empties the execution list, while a subsequent direct submission can undergo its own eligibility check at a later state. This explains the *recorded ordering of admission decisions* in the two fixed microcases more directly than interpreting a numerical waiting-time residual alone. The observation does **not** establish an undocumented simulator defect, a universally violated FIFO contract, actual physical CPU execution at list admission, an isolated causal effect of event timing, or a unique explanation for the 24-case A2 discrepancy. Four negative controls did not exhibit the **complete sequence**, without proving the sequence impossible elsewhere. The independently executed event-order reconstruction and source-controlled trace are documented in the [eligibility-order audit](SIMPAT_A1_EXECUTED_ELIGIBILITY_ORDER_PRECONDITION_VERIFIED_2026-11-18.md) and its [GitHub Actions verification](https://github.com/afshinyaghoobi/casper-iiot-cloudsim/actions/runs/44365200058).



## S6. In-sample predeclared rescan intervention (from v2.3 §5.10)


To move beyond purely observational event-order analysis, we froze a narrow native Java counterfactual protocol **before implementing or executing the treatment**. The six A1 workloads, including the two cases already known to exhibit younger-direct-EXEC/older-WAIT list membership, were reused **without changing arrival times or requested service**. A new Java17 native CloudSimPlus 8.5.7 subclass implements a binary `CONTROL` versus `TREATMENT` arm with identical simulation configurations and one previously defined intervention: after the normal `super.updateProcessing(now, share)` has returned, when `EXEC` is empty and `WAIT` nonempty, the treatment calls the native waiting-promotion method once more through the read-only traced wrapper. Its return event is compared with the original next-event result and the earlier positive value returned. This changes the time at which the scheduler may reconsider waiting work; it does **not** edit the CloudSimPlus library or directly modify queue lists. The intervention is a **scheduling-behavior counterfactual**, not an alternative physical execution model validated as correct.

Both arms completed all six fixed cases (18 jobs per arm). The new `CONTROL` native trace was **exactly byte-identical** to the independently executed historical A1 passive waiting-promotion control, and repeated executions of both arms were separately byte-identical. The predeclared primary indicator—direct admission of younger job 2 into `EXEC` while older job 1 remained in `WAIT`—occurred in **both previously positive cases** under CONTROL (`ORIGINAL_5MS`, `LATE10_5MS`), but **in neither case under TREATMENT**. No originally negative case acquired the same admission-list pattern. The treatment performed **12 added post-update rescan calls**, two per original case, and each case exhibited an observed promotion of the old waiting job within one of those calls.

In both previously positive cases, the first treatment rescan at native clock **1013.099623 ms** moved job 1 from the waiting list to the execution list immediately following the original processing update; under the untouched baseline, job 1 was instead promoted later (1037.2200236 ms in `ORIGINAL_5MS` and 1058.099623 ms in `LATE10_5MS`). Across all 12 treatment rescan calls, the actual **returned next-event time equaled the original** `super.updateProcessing` return *for that callback*, including **0.005 s** in each of the two critical first rescan calls. This rules out a changed **immediate return value** as the direct difference at that callback. It does **not** guarantee that the complete downstream native event schedule remained unchanged; changed queue membership can change later service accounting and event timing.

These results supply *locally controlled intervention evidence* that an additional post-update re-evaluation of waiting work is **sufficient to remove the particular list-ordering pattern in both pre-known fixed configurations**. They do not isolate a unique underlying Java branch, demonstrate a CloudSimPlus defect or violated documented FIFO contract, identify actual physical CPU starts, explain all 24 A2 analytical FIFO differences, or establish a general method for real IoT scheduling simulation. The fixtures were selected because the anomaly was already known: **there is no independent held-out validation**, and the treatment deliberately changes the scheduling semantics. The original control, complete revised trace, preregistration, full six-case outcomes and both unsuccessful-first-run schema correction and later **successful GitHub Actions run** are documented in the [native intervention report](SIMPAT_A1_PREREG_POSTUPDATE_RESCAN_INTERVENTION_VERIFIED_2026-11-18.md) and [verified CI output](https://github.com/afshinyaghoobi/casper-iiot-cloudsim/actions/runs/44381766155).



## S7. Eight newly specified input fixtures and the negative directional hypothesis (from v2.3 §5.11)


To limit dependence on the six previously inspected A1 microfixtures, we registered **eight different deterministic input configurations before the first native Java execution**, comprising four newly specified triples of requested job lengths and arrival times crossed with 1-μs and 5-ms simulation minimum-event intervals. The original SpaceShared CloudSimPlus 8.5.7 scheduler was used **without the rescan intervention** and the same passive BEFORE/AFTER instrumentation was retained. Across all eight cases, all **24 tasks completed** and duplicated native runs produced byte-identical full traces. These are independently configured synthetic cases, **not a probability sample, real IoT data or an independent deployment test**.

The prespecified source-event predicate `P` was evaluated strictly from events **before** the later job-2 admission decision: an earlier `updateProcessing` had attempted waiting promotion with job 0 in `EXEC`, job 1 in `WAIT` and job-1 eligibility returning false; the update ended with `EXEC` empty and job 1 still waiting; no intervening promotion removed job 1; immediately before job-2 submission, `EXEC` remained empty and job 1 remained in `WAIT`. Crucially, neither the job-2 eligibility return nor its `SUBMIT AFTER` state entered `P`. The separately observed outcome label was whether that submission moved job 2 into `EXEC` while job 1 remained waiting.

In the new fixed cases, `P` was true in **H_B_1US and H_B_5MS** and false in the six remaining cases. The matching admission event occurred in the same two cases and not in the other six: **two true positives, zero false positives, six true negatives and zero false negatives in eight designed fixtures**. This is a descriptive within-study agreement, *not* an estimated general prediction accuracy: `P` includes the immediately preceding execution-list availability and therefore may simply operationalize the documented native eligibility rule. Indeed, the independent **input-only** directional hypothesis that 5-ms minimum-event intervals would produce this state more frequently than 1-μs intervals **failed**: both groups contained exactly **one positive case out of four**. Thus the earlier six-case observation cannot justify an unqualified monotone event-gap explanation.

The complete unchanged case inputs, pre-execution predictions and falsification gates are in the [frozen independent-input protocol](SIMPAT_A1_EIGHTCASE_UNSEEN_INPUT_PREDICTION_PREREG_2026-11-18.md); all eight outcomes, including the failed hypothesis, are in the [source-verified new-input report](SIMPAT_EIGHT_NEW_INPUT_PREDICTION_AND_FAILED_GAP_HYPOTHESIS_2026-11-18.md) and [successful genuine Java execution](https://github.com/afshinyaghoobi/casper-iiot-cloudsim/actions/runs/44391376042). These findings narrow the empirical phase-state description in a few new original-native simulator fixtures but do not demonstrate a new validation algorithm, independent real-IoT accuracy or a CloudSimPlus defect.
