## Cloud-edge scheduling and feedback-based resource allocation
Cloud and cloud-edge schedulers often improve responsiveness by adapting heuristic decisions to resource measurements rather than solving a completely new class of mathematical optimization problems. Wu et al. [Wu2026DFAQLB] propose dynamic feedback-driven, queue-aware cloud load balancing through elastic queue control and multi-dimensional virtual-machine health assessment. Their evaluation uses an extended CloudSim model and multiple classical comparators. In contrast, our current experimental problem explicitly separates **CPU-finish observation time** from **end-to-end delivery time** of network outcomes, so a policy cannot receive either observation before its corresponding delivery event. This timing distinction is an engineering design choice whose benefit must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

Lei et al. [Lei2026AUHS] propose an adaptive heuristic for container scheduling in cloud-edge serverless workflows, coordinating task ordering, subdeadline assignment and execution planning. Their study evaluates deadline satisfaction, user satisfaction and scheduling overhead over a broad range of simulated workload conditions and comparisons with several heuristic and learning-based algorithms. Our target workload is instead heterogeneous IIoT task dispatch with sparse high-priority missions and delayed resource feedback. Although AUHS is a strong **experimental reporting template**, directly calling its original algorithm an executable baseline for our task model would be misleading without reproducing its workflow and container semantics.

## Related scheduling objectives and representations
Abdellatief [Abdellatief2026NSGA3WS] adapts NSGA-III for workflow scheduling under deadline, budget, energy and resource-utilization objectives, showing that problem-specific objective design and experimentally validated tradeoffs are a plausible engineering contribution. Farabegoli et al. [Farabegoli2026IDHGQL] integrate heterogeneous graph representations, aggregate computing and deep Q-learning for collective cloud-edge offloading, addressing cross-device effects and infrastructure load rather than the same CPU-finish/E2E observation protocol. Tao and Liu [TaoLiu2026Colocated] study scheduling interference between latency-sensitive and batch workloads in co-located resource clusters and investigate reclamation of underutilized resources. Their resource-contention framing is particularly relevant to testing whether our own background task rerouting unexpectedly harms mission deadlines.

Ayadi et al. [Ayadi2026PriorityDRL] develop a dual-agent, deadline-aware GPU scheduling framework with explicit job selection, allocation, preemption and sensitivity analysis using GPU traces. Their paper is relevant to the evaluation of deadline miss tradeoffs, ablations and scalability; it is not a directly transferable IIoT edge-routing implementation.

## Candidate technical distinction (hypothesis, NOT proven novelty)
We investigate a computationally lightweight, feedback-aware dispatch rule in which CPU-service errors become visible at CPU completion, while network excess becomes visible only at completed end-to-end feedback events. We evaluate separate CPU-only, network-only and combined routing rules, and explicitly test whether rerouting lower-priority traffic interferes with high-priority MISSION deadlines. Our Stage37 source development trial shows **no consistent advantage** (and worse MISSION deadline misses during one compute-shock test), so we do NOT claim an established performance contribution yet. A later manuscript can argue a specific incremental design contribution only if revised scheduling decisions improve actual mission/deadline metrics over independent paired workloads and competitive baselines.
