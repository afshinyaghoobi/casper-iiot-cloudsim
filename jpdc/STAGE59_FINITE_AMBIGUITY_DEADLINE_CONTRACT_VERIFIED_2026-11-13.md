## Decision rule and conditional guarantee
Let finite user-supplied hypothetical worlds w assign a complete 21-node positive end-to-end cost vector c_{wj} at the current decision point. Use positive task relative deadline D, nominal predictions p_j.
- m_j = max_w 1[c_{wj}>D] (0 iff every given hypothetical world predicts deadline safety)
- r_j = max_w(c_{wj}-min_k c_{wk}) (worst possible *latency* regret against a world-aware oracle **inside the imaginary matrix**).
- j* = lex argmin_j (m_j,r_j,p_j,j).

**Mathematical model-conditional feasibility proposition:** If at least one node has c_{wj}<=D for all hypothetical w, the selector picks some such node. Proof: its m_j=0 beats every m_j=1, and no m_j<0 exists; conversely if all m_j=1 then no model-robust feasible node exists. This is a property of finite matrix inputs, **not** a probabilistic or physical deadline guarantee.

**No-free-lunch counterexample:** Nominal world A costs for two nodes (10,12); alternative hidden world B costs (40,12); deadline D=20; identical issue-time observations. Nominal greedy chooses j0, deadline-minimax-regret chooses j1. Under A, j0 has lower physical latency 10 vs 12; under B j1 has 12 vs 40. No deterministic causal rule can know which hidden world is true from identical observations and optimize both. A randomized rule chooses j0 with probability p; expected regrets against world-aware best are 2(1-p) and 28p. Their maximum is minimized at p=1/15, yielding 28/15 ms: positive irreducible worst expected regret under this toy ambiguity. This does not establish any original CASPER lower bound, novelty claim or actual-performance improvement.
