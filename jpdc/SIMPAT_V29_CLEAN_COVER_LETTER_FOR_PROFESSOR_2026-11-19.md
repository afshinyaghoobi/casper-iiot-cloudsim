# Cover letter — Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory

**To:** The Editor, *Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory* (Elsevier)

**Subject:** Submission of “Queue-Wait and Execution-Time Semantics in CloudSimPlus 8.5.7: Implications for IoT Scheduling Simulation”

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Queue-Wait and Execution-Time Semantics in CloudSimPlus 8.5.7: Implications for IoT Scheduling Simulation,” for publication in *Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory*.

The study examines how CloudSimPlus 8.5.7 SpaceShared waiting-time and execution-duration measurements compare with an explicitly defined, capacity-matched, event-independent FIFO reference. Our reproducible evidence comprises a predeclared 24-condition factorial experiment with 288 completed synthetic CPU-only Cloudlets, six native admission microcases and subsequent control-based event instrumentation, and eight newly specified diagnostic fixtures.

Across the 24 conditions, the largest within-case absolute discrepancy in waiting time reaches 246 ms. The simulator and analytical reference disagree on 86/288 and 107/288 synthetic deadline classifications at the two specified thresholds. An after-completion duration-accounting sensitivity check and a timestamp-origin audit help distinguish the constructs being compared. We report a negative result for an independent input-only event-gap hypothesis rather than interpreting it as support for our expectation.

We believe that explicit characterization of simulator event and time-accounting semantics, together with source-linked reproducibility evidence, is relevant to the journal's interests in simulation methodology, verification, and model comparison. We make no claim that the analytical reference is physical ground truth, that a simulator defect has been proved, or that the synthetic results establish deployed IoT performance.

Thank you for considering our work.
