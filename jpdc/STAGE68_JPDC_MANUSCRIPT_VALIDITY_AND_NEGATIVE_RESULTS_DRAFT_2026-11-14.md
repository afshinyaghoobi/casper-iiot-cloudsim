## Proposed manuscript section: Simulation assumptions and fidelity

The evaluation uses a reproducible synthetic cloud-edge IIoT workload, with heterogeneous compute and network service parameters, online task arrivals and deadline-constrained scheduling. A Python event-driven implementation models node-local strict first-come, first-served (FCFS) CPU queues and delivery-time network feedback. We treat this model as a **defined experimental abstraction**, not as a drop-in replication of the Java CloudSimPlus 8.5.7 `CloudletSchedulerSpaceShared` runtime. This separation matters when evaluating online decisions whose effectiveness depends on the predicted queue wait.

A preregistered earlier 30,000-task cross-runtime comparison did not meet its queue-wait fidelity tolerance: the maximum discrepancy was 9.12140093294972 ms against a prespecified 5 ms gate. Subsequent source inspection identified a CloudSimPlus datacenter processing-update path that applies a minimum nonzero update delay, and native queue-list traces provided evidence that jobs need not always enter the execution list in strict submission order for the controlled configurations tested. A later independent six-case native experiment varied the requested CPU service durations by factors 0.5, 1 and 2, with simulation minimum event time 1 microsecond or 5 milliseconds. All 18 jobs completed; the maximum absolute differences between native reported wait and an independent ideal chronological FCFS reference were, respectively, 0, 25.950, 35.273, 24.120, 20.542 and 35.539 ms. A direct queue-list admission bypass was observed in one of the six configurations. These microbenchmarks reveal configuration sensitivity but neither prove that every CloudSim run is non-FCFS nor establish cross-runtime equivalence. The state of a scheduler's execution list is not necessarily a direct measurement of the instant at which physical CPU service begins.

The scientific implications are twofold. First, any claim about deadline misses, waiting time or relative scheduler advantage under the Python abstraction must be scoped to that abstraction until a native or independent event-semantics validation is performed. Second, clock granularity, admission order and service completion accounting should be treated as controlled factors rather than silently assumed to be equivalent across simulation libraries. A separate original Java configuration with unfinished tasks also remained unresolved; we do not suppress that configuration when assessing trace completeness.

## Proposed manuscript section: Causal observation and partial feedback

At dispatch time, a scheduler may observe its current nominal cost estimates and causally delivered historical CPU and end-to-end (E2E) network feedback. It cannot observe the future service duration or the unchosen candidate nodes' counterfactual execution outcomes. Our feedback constructor therefore uses only observations delivered before the current decision time, rejects retroactive or duplicate feedback and explicitly marks missing or stale node histories. An observed-subset criterion was investigated as a feasibility diagnostic, requiring at least two locally observed nodes while retaining the global nominal-greedy candidate within the eligible subset.

The strict full-network requirement could not be satisfied at any of 4,840 MISSION arrivals on its two development source seeds. A distinct restricted-observation study on separate development seeds found 4,349 of 4,532 MISSION arrivals eligible for subset-restricted consideration, with appreciable policy-, seed- and post-disturbance variation. These results quantify availability of information under the specific synthetic sources; they are not estimates of a universal ability to infer costs for all nodes, and they are not a paired treatment comparison because the studies used different seed cohorts. The missing node outcomes are policy dependent and cannot be considered missing at random.

Historical positive-residual sample maxima were also audited prospectively relative to observed chosen-node outcomes. In the tested post-disturbance source deployments, previously frozen maxima contained both CPU and network component residuals in 207/212, 200/204, 208/212 and 208/212 eligible MISSION outcomes for the four scenario-policy strata. These are **descriptive hit fractions** on actually chosen nodes under the evaluated policies, not conformal, distribution-free or uniform confidence guarantees. A high chosen-node fraction does not certify a hypothetical cost scenario for an unchosen node. Thus, the uncertainty sets remain heuristic and model conditional.

## Proposed manuscript section: Independent policy evaluation and negative findings

We explored a partial-observation minimax-regret decision variant that changes the route of a MISSION task only when its observed-subset conditions hold and otherwise falls back to the original CASPER_FULL policy. Non-MISSION tasks retain the original routing decision. A two-source development screen showed encouraging post-disturbance results, but one development seed exhibited increased full-period MISSION misses. Accordingly, we froze the code and assessed the candidate on eight new independent source seeds, using the original CASPER_FULL and PRED_GREEDY as controls.

The independently preregistered holdout did **not** pass its safety criteria. Across the eight source seeds, post-disturbance MISSION misses under the CPU disturbance totaled 24 for CASPER_FULL and 17 for the variant, yet the new policy incurred one extra miss in each of two individual seeds. For the network disturbance, the pooled counts were 31 and 14, respectively. Source-level exact two-sided paired sign-test p values were 1.000 (CPU) and 0.125 (network); neither establishes statistically significant superiority at the usual 0.05 threshold. Furthermore, the variant produced more full-period MISSION misses than CASPER_FULL in six seed-by-disturbance cells. These prespecified safety failures prohibit promoting this candidate, notwithstanding favorable aggregate descriptive changes. Counting each task or each policy trajectory as an independent replicate would inflate the nominal sample size; the independent source seed, not the task, is the appropriate unit of this paired evaluation.

A subsequent **retrospective**, outcome-selected task trace study documented examples in which the variant's observed execution trajectory had longer queue wait, CPU service or network delay than the original policy. The paired traces compare distinct, dynamically evolving policy trajectories and therefore do not identify the isolated causal effect of changing one task's destination. The retrospective examples do not repair the independent holdout failure and were not treated as a new sample.

## Threats to validity

**Construct and model validity.** CPU and network service are represented by synthetic distributions and scenario-specific hidden perturbations; task deadlines and their distributions are experimental constructs. No independently acquired and provenance-checked factory task trace currently validates their prevalence or joint dynamics in a real deployment. The abstract strict-FCFS Python queue and native CloudSimPlus SpaceShared scheduler demonstrably differ in controlled tests. The reported simulator metrics should not be translated into physical-device guarantees, field reliability, production-level service guarantees or measured energy savings without further validation.

**Internal validity and information leakage.** Feedback handlers admit only delivered CPU and E2E measurements and protect against timestamp rollback. These software invariants reduce one class of lookahead errors but cannot remove selection bias: policies determine which nodes obtain fresh feedback. Counterfactual node performance remains censored. Historical sample maxima may understate nonstationary extremes. Reproducible execution and unit-test success do not imply that the physical model is correct.

**Statistical validity.** Multiple tasks, shocks and policy runs reuse each source seed, so observations are correlated. The principal prospective candidate assessment uses eight independent original source seeds, not hundreds of thousands of independent experimental units. Small n, ties and multiplicity constrain the power of source-level tests; isolated favorable descriptive counts must not be presented as a causal performance guarantee. No retrospectively modified objective, reduced safety gate or seed selection was used to recast the failed prospective evaluation as a success.

**External validity and baselines.** The findings concern the tested synthetic IIoT-like jobs, simulated service heterogeneity and two perturbation regimes. They do not yet provide Java-policy replication, real hardware evidence, external workload sensitivity, or complete faithful reproductions of all published comparator algorithms. Simplified queue-pressure comparators, when used, must be labeled as approximations and not attributed as faithful reproductions of their cited works.

## Safe candidate conclusion paragraph

These results indicate that causal feedback eligibility and source-level reproducibility are achievable in a controlled synthetic cloud-edge setting, but neither broad uncertainty coverage nor a uniformly safer partial-observation scheduler has been established. In particular, a frozen candidate with favorable pooled post-disturbance misses violated its prespecified per-source safety criterion, while native CloudSimPlus queue timing differed materially from the ideal strict-FCFS abstraction. Consequently, the current evidence supports a careful methodological account of observation constraints, simulation fidelity and failure modes, rather than a general claim of improved real-world deadline reliability. Future evaluation requires independently sourced workload traces, native or hardware-validated queue semantics, faithful baselines and a newly preregistered policy candidate with distinct development and confirmation sources.
